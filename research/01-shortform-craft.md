# The Craft of Short-Form Editing (Reels / TikTok / Shorts / LinkedIn) — Research Brief

Researched 2026-10-01. Sources at the end. **A note on how much to trust the numbers:** official platform statements (Mosseri, YouTube's Todd Sherman, TikTok Ads Help Center, DCMP captioning standards) are the most reliable. Many specific percentages come from vendor blogs (Opus, quso, Blitzcut, etc.) and are directional heuristics, not peer-reviewed. The rules below put **conservative, editor-consensus values** first and flag shaky numbers with "(vendor data)".

---

## 0. What the platforms actually reward (frame for every rule below)

- **Instagram Reels (Mosseri, Jan 2025):** the three top signals are **watch time**, **likes per reach** and **sends per reach**. Sends (DM shares) count most for reaching non-followers. Original content is favoured. Reposts and watermarked clips (TikTok/CapCut logos) lose cold distribution. Hashtags don't increase reach; keywords in the caption and on-screen text help discovery.
- **YouTube Shorts (Todd Sherman, YouTube):** **retention and replays** are the strongest success signals. The key metric is **"Viewed vs. Swiped Away"** (YouTube Studio > Reach). Top Shorts sit at **70–90% viewed** (Paddy Galloway's analysis). Below ~60% points to a hook problem.
- **TikTok (Ads Help Center, best practices updated 2025):** sound on, 9:16, ≥720p, everything inside the UI safe zone, **state the proposition in the first 3 s**, on-screen text at **5–10 words/sec max**. TikTok's "Creative Codes" research says native-looking content performs best.
- **LinkedIn:** autoplays **muted**, so the first 3 s have to work with no sound. 30–90 s vertical or 4:5 works well. Captions: sentence case, ≤6–8 words per line.
- **The general rule:** reach follows retention. Every editing choice should be judged by whether it keeps the next second watched, gets the video replayed, or makes someone send it to a friend.

## 1. Hooks (0–3 s)

### 1.1 Timing rules
- **The viewer decides in about 1 s. Assume you have 1.0–1.5 s, not 3.** Mosseri: "most users decide in less than a second." Eye-tracking-style claims put commitment at about 2.8–3.2 s, and 50–60% of all drop-off happens in the first 3 s (vendor data).
- **Target:** ≥70% still watching at the 3 s mark (Shorts "viewed vs swiped" ≥70%). Below 60% means rework the hook before touching anything else.
- **Start speaking on frame 1.** No lead-in breath, no "so…", no greeting. Trim to the first syllable, keeping at most 2–4 frames of pre-roll.
- **Something must move in frame 1** (the subject mid-gesture, an object entering, a camera push). A still first frame looks like a thumbnail ad.

### 1.2 Use all three hook layers at once (they stack)
1. **Verbal hook:** 5–12 words, spoken at full energy, front-loading the most specific noun or number. ("This one UTM mistake broke 40% of my reports.")
2. **Visual hook:** the payoff or the problem *visible* in frame 1: the result, the broken thing, the before state, a surprising object, a face showing emotion, or a hand doing something.
3. **Text hook:** 3–7 words on screen from frame 1. It should **complement the voice, not transcribe it** (e.g. VO says the claim, text names the topic or stakes). It must sit inside the safe zone and be readable in under 1 s.
- Sound-off viewers (about 50–70% on IG/LinkedIn) get the hook only from the visual and text layers, so those layers must make sense without audio.

### 1.3 Hook formulas (with examples)
| Formula | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mistake / negative | "You're doing X wrong" / "Stop doing X" | "Your UTM links are lying to you." |
| Specific result | number + outcome + timeframe | "This 8-second fix doubled our click data." |
| Curiosity gap | "Nobody tells you this about X…" | "There's one field in GA4 nobody checks." |
| Contrarian | challenge a held belief | "Link-in-bio is killing your traffic." |
| Direct callout | name the viewer | "If you run paid ads, watch this before Monday." |
| Visual proof first | show the result, then "here's how" | final dashboard on screen → "Here's how I built this in 2 minutes." |
| In-media-res story | start at the peak moment | "So the client calls me, furious…" |
| Comparison | X vs Y with a verdict implied | "utm_source vs utm_medium — most people swap them." |
| Question (pain-point specific) | a question only the target viewer feels | "Why does GA4 say 'direct' for your email traffic?" |
- Specific beats generic. Pain-point questions reportedly outperform generic openings by about 23% in retention (vendor data). Use real numbers, nouns and named tools.
- **Write 3–5 hook variants per video.** Test them with IG Trial Reels or by posting variants, and keep the one with the highest 3 s hold.

### 1.4 Pattern interrupts for the opening
- Start on a hard **punch-in** (100%→115%) on the first stressed word, an object thrown into frame, a whip-pan, an unexpected location or prop, or a mid-sentence cut-in.
- A one-frame-different visual (a colour shift or angle change) at 0.8–1.2 s re-catches the eye just as the viewer is about to swipe.

### 1.5 The first frame is your feed thumbnail
- On Shorts, the main feed shows the video itself, not a custom thumbnail; you can only pick a frame on mobile, and it appears in search, channel and subscriptions only. Reels covers are cropped to **3:4 (1080×1440)** on the profile grid, and a cover can't be changed after posting.
- Design frame 1 so it works as a still: a face or subject in the centre 1080×1440 area, 3–6 words of text, high contrast, and no motion blur.

### 1.6 Openings that lose viewers (avoid)
- **Logo or brand sting first:** it reads as an ad, so the swipe is instant.
- **Music-only or ambient intros:** no information arrives in the decision second.
- **Text-only title cards:** they read as a slide, with no human or motion cue.
- **Greetings and backstory:** "Hey guys", "So I've been thinking…", context before proof.
- **Slow fades from black:** the first 0.5 s is wasted.
- **Hooks that don't match the payoff:** they cause mid-video drop-off and train the algorithm against you.

## 2. Retention structure

### 2.1 Length targets (directional)
- Quick tip / hack: **15–25 s**. Tutorial / list: **25–45 s**. Story / BTS: **30–60 s**. Opinion or explainer with strong pacing: **45–90 s** (LinkedIn and IG tolerate 30–90 s).
- **Total watch time matters more than completion rate alone.** 40 s at 60% retention (24 s watched) beats 20 s at 70% (14 s), but only if the middle never sags.
- After the first cut, **cut a further 20–30%.** Every sentence must earn its place.

### 2.2 Re-hook cadence
- **Change something visually every 2–5 s** (a cut, punch-in, b-roll, graphic or text change). Never hold a static frame for more than about 5 s on talking-head content. High performers average one cut every 2–4 s (vendor data).
- **Give the story a new beat every 5–7 s:** a new fact, a turn ("but here's the problem…"), or a reveal.
- **Make verbal re-hooks every 10–15 s** in 30–90 s videos: "and the second one is worse", "this is where it gets weird", "the fix takes 10 seconds". The opus framework puts deliberate re-hooks at **about 15 s and about 30 s**.
- **Give a quick win in the first 10 s** (a trust deposit). Videos that pay something off in the first 15 s reportedly hold about 20% better (vendor data).

### 2.3 Open loops and payoff placement
- Open a loop in the hook ("the third one cost us $4k") and close it **no earlier than 60–70% of runtime**. Open a smaller loop before you close the big one.
- **Put the best moment at about ⅔ through,** late enough to reward people who stayed and early enough that most viewers are still there. Never save the only payoff for the last 2 s.
- In list formats, **tease the order** ("the last one is the one nobody does") and put the strongest item last or second-to-last.

### 2.4 Arc templates
- **15 s:** Hook (0–2) → Proof or demo (2–10) → Payoff and loop line (10–15). One idea only.
- **30 s:** Hook (0–2) → Stakes / why it matters (2–6) → Steps or points 1–2 (6–20, re-hook at about 12) → Payoff (20–27) → Soft CTA or loop (27–30).
- **45 s:** Hook (0–2) → Quick win (2–10) → Build with 2–3 beats, re-hooks at about 15 and 30 → Twist or best point at about 30 → Payoff (35–42) → CTA (42–45).
- **60 s:** Hook → Context in ≤5 s → 3 beats of about 12 s each, each opening with its own mini-hook → Climax at about 40 s → Resolution → CTA or loop in the final 3 s.
- **90 s (LinkedIn / IG long Reels):** as for 60 s, but with a mid-point reset at about 45 s ("Now here's the part that actually matters") and a fresh visual mode (a new location, screen recording or graphic) for the second half.

### 2.5 Looping endings (replays carry heavy weight on Shorts and TikTok)
- **Sentence loop:** the last line runs into the first line ("…and that's exactly why" → "your UTM data is lying to you").
- **Visual match loop:** the last frame matches the first frame's composition, colour or motion. Cutting on an occlusion (a hand or object covering the lens) hides the seam.
- **Circular action / transformation loop:** end on the result you opened with.
- **End abruptly on the payoff word, with no hold.** A clean hard end prompts a replay. (Exception: when the platform needs it, hold the final frame 1–2 s for a readable end-state.)
- Even a 10% replay rate noticeably boosts distribution (vendor data).

## 3. Pacing and cutting

### 3.1 Silence and filler removal
- **Silence threshold:** treat audio below about −30 to −40 dBFS lasting longer than the minimum gap as silence.
  - Social talking head: cut gaps longer than **0.3–0.5 s**. Explainer with weight: about 0.5–0.7 s. Podcast or emotional story: about 0.7–1.0 s.
- **Padding:** keep **0.08–0.15 s (about 2–4 frames at 30 fps)** before each word onset and **0.1–0.2 s** after the word tail. Clipping consonants (plosives, "s" tails) sounds robotic.
- **Breaths:** remove most of them. Keep about 1 in 4 before a big line, because breaths read as human and a perfectly breathless VO sounds synthetic. Alternatively, duck breaths −10 to −15 dB instead of cutting them.
- **Filler ("um", "uh", "like", "you know", "so", restarts, repeated words):** remove them all in tips and explainers. In story-led or personality content, keep the occasional natural "so" or laugh for authenticity.
- Don't remove every pause. **A deliberate 0.3–0.6 s pause before a punchline or reveal adds weight.** Keep it and hold on the face.

### 3.2 Jump cuts and punch-ins
- **Jump cuts are native to the format.** Hide the harsh ones by alternating framing: on every 2nd–3rd cut, scale **100% ↔ 110–120%** (punch-in). Reserve **130–150%** for emphasis beats (a punchline or key number). That needs 4K source or a sharp 1080p head-and-shoulders shot so it doesn't soften.
- When you scale, **re-centre on the eyes.** Keep the eyeline on the upper third at every scale step.
- **Use eased zooms** (a 2–6% slow push over 1–3 s) for tension or a "listen to this" moment. **Use hard punch-ins** for energy and emphasis. Don't stack both on the same line.
- **Alternate three framings** (wide 100% / medium 115% / tight 135%) so consecutive cuts never use the same scale.

### 3.3 J-cuts, L-cuts, cut-on-action
- **L-cut:** the speaker's audio continues over b-roll. This is the default way to insert b-roll without breaking the thought.
- **J-cut:** the next scene's audio (a sound effect, ambience, or the next speaker) starts **4–12 frames before** the picture cut. Use it for scene and location changes.
- **Cut on action:** cut mid-gesture, head turn or hand movement, because motion masks the cut. For a whip transition, cut at peak blur.
- **Cut on the beat or syllable:** put picture cuts on music transients or the stressed syllable of the VO. Being 1–2 frames early feels snappy; late feels sluggish.

### 3.4 B-roll rhythm
- In talking-head content, insert b-roll or graphics **every 4–8 s**, lasting **1.5–4 s each**. Return to the face for opinions, emotion and the CTA, because the face is what builds trust and follows.
- B-roll must be **literal and specific** to the words being spoken: show the exact thing, screen or number named. Generic stock "laptop typing" footage reads as filler and AI-ish.
- Mix modes: face → screen recording → close-up of a real object → graphic → face.

### 3.5 When NOT to cut
- During an **emotional or genuine moment** (a laugh, a pause, a reaction). Let it breathe for 1–2 s.
- During a **demonstration whose continuity is the proof**: a single take proves it isn't faked.
- When the **music phrase** is about to resolve: cut on the resolve, not just before it.
- **Over-cutting** (cuts under 1 s with no reason, and a sound effect on each) causes cognitive overload. The pace should vary: fast sections are noticeable only because slower ones exist.

## 4. Captions and on-screen text

### 4.1 Why and whether
- Most feed viewing is muted (often cited as 50–85%, and LinkedIn autoplays muted). Burned-in captions are reported to add **12–25% retention** (vendor data), and better-formatted captions beat auto-captions on LinkedIn by 40–60% watch time (vendor data).
- **Burned-in vs platform captions:** burn in for consistent styling and timing control across platforms. Also upload an SRT or enable the platform's auto-captions for accessibility and search indexing, since platforms read caption text. Don't show both at once: disable platform auto-captions when burning in.

### 4.2 Sizing for 1080×1920
- Body caption: **60–75 px** cap height (default about 65–70 px) in weight **700–900** sans. Emphasis words can go to about 80–95 px. Never go below 48 px or above about 100 px.
- **Stroke and shadow:** 3–6 px dark stroke, or a soft drop shadow (0 2 px 8 px, 40–60% black), or a semi-opaque pill behind the text. Aim for **≥4.5:1 contrast** against what's behind the text (WCAG AA).
- **Line width:** keep within about 900 px (the centre 80% of the width). At most **2 lines**, and **≤32 characters per line** (DCMP).

### 4.3 Chunking and timing
- **Use 2–5 words per chunk** for social. 1-word karaoke suits high-energy content only, and 6–8 words per line max on LinkedIn.
- **Break chunks at phrase boundaries** (noun phrase, verb phrase). Never split an article from its noun ("the / dashboard") or a number from its unit.
- **Sync chunk onset to the first word's audio onset,** ±2 frames. Leave each chunk up until the next one replaces it, with no blank flashes between chunks.
- **Reading rate:** about **150–180 wpm** for adult content (DCMP: 150–160 wpm; hard ceiling 225 wpm). Static overlay text (non-caption) must be on screen **≥2 s** or about 0.3 s per word, whichever is longer. TikTok's rule is 5–10 words/sec as an absolute ceiling.

### 4.4 Position vs platform UI (1080×1920)
- **Instagram Reels:** avoid the top **about 220–270 px** (username, audio label), the bottom **about 420–670 px** (caption, audio, the description expands), and the **right about 120–160 px** (the like/comment/share rail runs about 40% of the height from the bottom). Keep about 60–65 px clear at the sides.
- **TikTok:** top **about 140–150 px**, right **about 160 px**, bottom **about 320–480 px**. It varies with caption length and add-ons.
- **YouTube Shorts:** similar. Avoid the bottom **about 350–420 px** (title, channel, subscribe) and the right **about 120–190 px**.
- **Universal safe box:** text centred horizontally, **caption baseline about 1150–1350 px from the top** (lower-middle, roughly 60–70% down). Critical elements (faces, key text) go in the **centre 900×1340 px**, starting about 260 px from the top. This also survives IG's 3:4 grid crop.
- **Don't put captions over the mouth or eyes.** If the face is low in frame, move the captions to the upper-middle (about 400–550 px from the top).

### 4.5 Emphasis practices
- Highlight **at most 1 word per chunk, and only about 1 in every 3–4 chunks.** Choose the semantic keyword: a number, the outcome, or the "wrong" word.
- **Emphasis methods, from most restrained:** weight change → single brand accent colour → about 1.15× scale pop (a 3–5 frame ease-out) → underline or marker sweep. **Pick 1–2 methods per video and stay consistent.**
- Numbers and money go in digits ("40%", "$4k"), never spelled out.

### 4.6 What reads as "AI / template", and how to avoid it
- **Tells:** word-by-word ALL-CAPS Montserrat Black with yellow/green highlights on every other word (the "Hormozi" preset is now a commodity, and Hormozi himself dropped it); bounce or shake on every word; emojis inserted after keywords; every line tilted; a "pop" sound per word; perfectly uniform timing; captions centred over the face; auto-caption errors left in.
- **Alternatives that read as crafted:**
  - **Netflix-subtitle restraint:** sentence case, one consistent font, colour and position, white with a soft shadow, emphasis rare (Starter Story's creative director: "keep sh*t simple… readable").
  - **Native-app captions** (IG, TikTok, Edits): TikTok's Creative Codes say native-looking content wins.
  - **A custom on-brand system:** a non-default typeface (a brand font or a characterful grotesk or serif), the brand accent colour used only for emphasis, and one signature entrance animation.
  - **Proofread every caption.** Fix brand names, numbers and casing by hand, because an auto-caption error is the clearest sign of low effort.

## 5. Sound

### 5.1 Loudness targets
- **Final master:** integrated **−14 LUFS** (safe across YouTube, Shorts, TikTok and IG, which all normalise around −14). For Reels and TikTok feeds you can push to **−13 to −12 LUFS** so you aren't quieter than neighbouring videos. **True peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP.**
- **Dialogue:** normalise speech to about **−16 to −14 LUFS** (short-term). Use light compression (2:1–4:1 ratio, 3–6 dB of gain reduction), a high-pass at about 80–100 Hz, de-ess at about 5–8 kHz, and a limiter at −1 dBTP.
- **Mobile speakers** have almost no bass, so check the mix on a phone speaker. Presence at 2–5 kHz carries intelligibility.

### 5.2 Music bed and ducking
- **Under speech:** music sits **18–25 dB below the voice.** A practical starting point is VO at about −16 LUFS and music at about −30 to −34 LUFS while talking. WCAG 1.4.7 asks for background ≥20 dB below foreground speech.
- **Ducking:** sidechain the music to the VO with **15–25 dB of depth, 10–30 ms attack and 200–500 ms release.** Bring the music up by 6–10 dB in gaps longer than 1 s and on intros or outros between lines.
- **Choose music without a strong lead melody or vocals** under the VO. Vocals fight speech intelligibility.
- **Use musical structure:** edit the music so a phrase change or drop lands on the key reveal, and end the video on a musical resolve or a hard stop, never a fade-out tail.

### 5.3 SFX norms
- **Keep it to 3–8 meaningful SFX moments per 30 s** (Sonilo recommends 3–5 for most Shorts). Most cuts should be silent handoffs. Before export, remove at least one SFX.
- **Level:** SFX peak about **6–12 dB below the voice peak.** Dip the music 3–6 dB under a whoosh with fast recovery. Test at low volume.
- **Timing:** whoosh length matches the transition length (a 0.5 s move gets a 0.5 s whoosh), with the peak landing on the cut frame. Pops and clicks land on the exact frame the text or element appears.
- **Match the type to the visual:** fast swipe/zoom → short whoosh with a sharp attack. Slow fade → no SFX (let the music breathe). Scene change → riser or sweep plus a soft impact. Number reveal → a subtle tick or soft hit. UI interaction → a realistic click.
- **Cheap tells:** the same whoosh on every cut, a pop on every caption word, cartoon or plasticky library sounds, and SFX louder than the voice. Varying the samples and layering in quiet room tone or foley sounds premium.

### 5.4 Silence as a tool
- **A 0.3–1.0 s music dropout** (cut the bed to zero) right before the key line or reveal is the strongest emphasis device available, and it works better than any SFX. Use it once per video.
- Keep a bed of room tone under VO cuts so jump cuts don't produce digital-silence "holes".

### 5.5 Trending audio
- **TikTok and IG:** trending sounds help discovery for trend-format content. Add them in-app (or at about 3–10% volume under the VO) to get the audio-page association.
- **For brand or business content, use licensed or commercial-library music.** Commercial accounts don't have access to the full music library. Original audio or your own voice is favoured as "original content" on IG.

## 6. Visual polish

### 6.1 Colour and grade for phone footage
- **Order:** (1) Convert LOG to Rec.709 with the correct LUT if shot in Apple Log or another LOG format. (2) Balance: white balance on a neutral object, neutral blacks with RGB aligned at the bottom of the parade. (3) Set exposure so **skin sits at about 55–70 IRE**. (4) Contrast: a gentle S-curve. (5) Look: subtle (a teal push in shadows or warmth in mids at 10–20% strength).
- **Protect skin tones:** keep them on the vectorscope skin line and pull back saturation before they go orange. A light grade beats a heavy LUT. Heavy teal-orange, crushed blacks and over-sharpening read as amateur or filtered.
- **Match every clip** in a video to the same white balance and exposure. Inconsistent grade between cuts is a top tell of a cheap edit.
- **Export:** 1080×1920 (or 2160×3840 if the platform accepts it), 30 fps (or 24/25 for a cinematic feel; match the source), H.264 high profile, about 15–25 Mbps. Avoid upscaled low-res sources.

### 6.2 Framing for 9:16
- **Eyes on the upper third** (about 600–700 px from the top on 1920), framed from mid-chest up, with modest headroom (the head top about 250–350 px from the frame top, so it clears the UI).
- **Keep the lower ~35% clean** for captions and UI. Keep key action out of the right ~15% (the button rail).
- **Look into the lens,** not at the screen. The camera should be at eye level. If you aren't on a phone, use a long-ish lens (≥50 mm equivalent) for flattering face geometry.
- **Backgrounds** are more visible in vertical video: use a simple background with depth, a practical light, and a subject separated from the wall by ≥1 m.
- **Reframe 16:9 sources** by tracking the subject, not by centre-cropping. Never letterbox 16:9 inside 9:16 with blurred bars unless the style is deliberate.

### 6.3 B-roll sourcing (in order of preference)
1. **Your own footage:** real product, office, hands, screen recordings (at 2× scale so UI text is readable at about 40 px+).
2. **Recreated or illustrated UI and diagrams** made for the video.
3. **Licensed stock,** used only when it is very specific, graded to match, and on screen for 1–2 s.
4. Avoid generic AI-generated b-roll for factual claims. It is recognisable and erodes trust.

### 6.4 Motion graphics: premium vs cheap
- **Premium:** eased motion (expo or cubic out on entrances, about 8–15 frames, never linear), one consistent motion language, restrained palette (1 accent + neutrals), elements that **enter from where they belong** (anchored to the subject or UI), subtle overshoot of at most 5–10%, about 3–6 frames of stagger between items, generous whitespace, one idea per card.
- **Cheap:** linear moves, default template transitions (spin, cube, glitch, flash) on every cut, more than 3 fonts, drop-shadow-heavy 3D text, motion on everything simultaneously, stock lower-thirds, emojis or stickers as decoration, and gradient-rainbow text.
- **Transitions:** use **mostly hard cuts.** Motivated transitions (match cut, whip on movement, occlusion wipe with a hand or object) should make up at most about 1 in 5 cuts.

### 6.5 Signals that a video is not AI-made
- **Real human presence:** the real voice, the real face for at least part of the video, real locations, and a hand interacting with a real object.
- **Imperfection kept in:** a natural laugh, a slight handheld drift, an unscripted aside, a breath before the big line. Lo-fi or native-feeling creative reportedly beats polished ad creative on engagement (vendor data), because it doesn't trigger the "this is an ad" filter.
- **Restraint:** fewer effects, fewer SFX, fewer emphasis colours, and a consistent system.
- **Specificity:** real numbers, real screens, real names, and opinions with an edge. Generic statements and stock visuals are the strongest "AI slop" signals.
- **Avoid the AI script tells:** "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "game-changer", "unlock", tidy groups of three, rhetorical-question-then-answer used in every line, and identical sentence lengths.

## 7. CTAs and endings

### 7.1 Types
- **Soft CTA** (best default for reach): a one-line, value-framed prompt woven into the payoff. ("Save this before your next campaign.") Saves and sends are ranking signals, so "save this" and "send this to the person who builds your UTMs" serve both the viewer and the algorithm.
- **Send-bait CTA** (Instagram): "Send this to your [role/friend] who…". This directly targets **sends per reach**, the top signal for reaching non-followers.
- **Comment-keyword CTA** (lead generation): "Comment **GUIDE** and I'll DM you the template", automated with ManyChat or similar. It costs the viewer one tap instead of two-plus for link-in-bio, the comments boost ranking, and the link lands in the DM. One reported case got about 6× the clicks of a link-in-bio CTA (anecdotal). Show the keyword on screen in caps for at least 2 s **and** say it out loud.
- **Follow CTA:** works best when it's specific and tied to a series ("Follow for part 2" or "I post one of these every Tuesday"). Generic "like and subscribe" adds little.
- **Hard CTA** ("Sign up at…", "link in bio"): only for conversion-goal or ad content, kept to one action, at most about 3 s, spoken and on screen.

### 7.2 Placement and form
- **Put the CTA in the last 2–4 s**, spoken in the creator's voice over live footage. Don't use a separate end card. For long Reels you can also seed a light CTA mid-video right after the payoff.
- **No outro cards, logo stings or "thanks for watching" frames.** In short form they create a dead tail that lowers average percentage viewed and breaks the loop. If branding is needed, use a small persistent logo or handle in a safe corner, or a brand-coloured caption system.
- **Brand end cards only for paid ads** where the platform or brand requires them, kept to at most 1.5 s, and still with a voice line over them.
- **For looping videos,** let the CTA be the bridge back into the start (sentence loop), or put the CTA before the final payoff line so the video ends on the payoff.

## 8. Common mistakes that kill retention

1. **A slow first second:** a logo, greeting, music-only or text-only opening, a fade from black, or a breath before the first word.
2. **The hook promises something the video doesn't deliver,** or the payoff is delayed to the last frame. This causes a mid-video cliff and worse distribution.
3. **Context before proof:** explaining backstory or who you are before showing why the viewer should care.
4. **More than 5 s of visual stasis,** or a middle with no re-hook and no new beat.
5. **Dead air and filler left in** (gaps longer than 0.5 s, "um", restarts), or the opposite: zero breathing room and machine-gun cuts with an SFX on each.
6. **Text in the UI dead zones** (bottom caption area, right rail, top bar), or captions covering the face or mouth.
7. **Unreadable text:** under about 48 px, thin weights, no stroke or shadow over busy footage, more than 2 lines, or text up for less than 2 s.
8. **Template caption presets with errors,** emoji spam and highlights on every word.
9. **Audio problems:** a quiet master (under −16 LUFS), music fighting the VO, harsh or untreated room echo, clipped peaks, or mismatched levels between clips.
10. **An outro card or slow fade at the end:** it kills completion and loops.
11. **Watermarks from other apps** (TikTok or CapCut logos on Reels) or reposted footage, which IG suppresses.
12. **Horizontal footage letterboxed** in 9:16, or a lazy centre crop that cuts off heads or UI.
13. **Inconsistent grade and white balance between cuts,** or over-filtered skin.
14. **Too many ideas:** one short should have one idea and one takeaway.
15. **Running too long for the content:** about 20 s of substance stretched to 60 s. Cut the draft by about 30%.
16. **Generic stock or AI b-roll** that doesn't literally match the words.

## Quick-reference numbers (for automated pipelines)
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Speech starts | frame 1 (≤4 frames pre-roll) |
| Hook text | 3–7 words, readable in <1 s |
| 3 s hold target | ≥70% (Shorts viewed-vs-swiped ≥70%) |
| Visual change | every 2–5 s; never >5 s static |
| Narrative beat / re-hook | every 5–7 s; verbal re-hook every 10–15 s |
| Silence cut threshold | >0.3–0.5 s @ < −35 dBFS; pad 2–4 f pre / 3–6 f post |
| Punch-in scale | 110–120% routine; 130–150% emphasis |
