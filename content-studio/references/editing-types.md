# 02 — Every Video Type / Format and How a Pro Edits Each

Research compiled 2026-10-01 for an autonomous video-production skill. Sources are mostly 2024–2026 platform documentation, agency playbooks, creator-tool documentation and editor guides (full list at the end). Numbers are working defaults, not laws. Where sources disagree, the more conservative value is given.

---

## 0. Universal rules (these apply to every type below)

**Platform length and spec defaults**

| Platform / placement | Aspect | Sweet-spot length | Hard limits / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok organic | 9:16, 1080×1920 | 15–45s organic; **21–34s for In-Feed ads** (1 in 4 top ads sit in that range, +1.6% impressions) | Over 93% of top videos have sound. Safe zone: keep clear of the top ~150px, the right ~120px and the bottom ~350–400px |
| Instagram Reels | 9:16 | 30–90s; reach drops past ~90s even though 3 min is allowed (raised Jan 2025) | UI covers the top ~14% and the bottom ~35%. Keep critical content in the centre 50–65%. Design for sound on AND sound off |
| YouTube Shorts | 9:16 | 15–30s gets the highest retention (often >80%); up to 3 min allowed | The swipe-away rate is the key negative signal. Loops and replays are weighted heavily |
| YouTube long-form | 16:9 | 8–20 min typical; 15–30 min for deep content | Master to −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. Use chapters |
| LinkedIn organic | 9:16 or 4:5 (vertical feed since 2024–25) | 30–90s | Mostly watched sound-off at work, so burned-in captions are mandatory |
| LinkedIn ads | 4:5 recommended (1080×1350), 1:1, 16:9 | **<15s** for most successful ads | Accepts SRT captions. MP4, ≤200MB |
| Meta (FB/IG) paid | 9:16 for Reels/Stories, 4:5 for Feed | 15–30s | The first 3s decide performance; the scroll-stop window is ~1.5s |

**The universal beat sheet (short form):** Hook (0–1.5/3s) → Context or stakes (≤5s) → Value or journey → Payoff (land it within the first 80% of runtime) → Soft CTA or loop-back. Long form adds a "contract" at 0:15–0:45 and re-hooks at about 25%, 50% and 75%.

**Universal cut-rhythm defaults:** change the frame or visual every 3–7s in explanatory sections, every 1–2s at high-energy or punchline moments, and allow 8–12s holds for genuinely compelling unbroken moments. Hold a complex shot at least 3s and a simple one 1–2s. Cutting consistently faster than about 1s causes overload. Vary the density: uniform pacing produces the "steady decline" retention curve.

**Universal audio defaults:** dialogue is king. Music bed sits 12–20 dB under dialogue peaks (duck 6–12 dB with sidechain attack 30–80ms and release 250–700ms, or −15 to −20 dB manual). Raise music only in gaps and in pure B-roll. Sound-designed transitions (whoosh, flash, shake) at most once every 20–40s unless the format is explicitly high-energy. Master to −14 LUFS.

**Universal caption defaults:** about 80% of Reels plays are sound-off and LinkedIn is mostly muted, so burn in captions on every talking video. Use 1–4 words per caption chunk for short-form "karaoke" captions (active-word highlight), and 3–7 words per frame for kinetic type. Never use thin font weights. Keep captions inside the safe zone (lower-middle third, above the bottom ~20–35% UI band). Don't let captions and graphics compete for the same screen space.

**Universal mistakes:** a logo, branding or "hey guys" before value; restating the title; one static angle for 10+ minutes; generic stock B-roll that adds motion but not meaning; a CTA hidden under platform UI; overused text emphasis (it trains the eye to ignore it); grading only on a calibrated monitor instead of checking on a phone.

## 1. Talking-head (founder / creator)

- **Purpose:** authority, personality and trust. It is the cheapest high-trust format.
- **Length:** 30–60s for Shorts/Reels/TikTok, 45–90s for LinkedIn, 8–15 min for a YouTube essay.
- **Beat sheet:** spoken hook in the first second (a claim, a contrarian take, or a "you're doing X wrong") → why it matters (1 line) → 2–3 points, each with proof or an example → the takeaway, restated in one memorable line → a soft CTA or a question to drive comments.
- **Shot grammar:** an A-cam medium close-up with eyes on the upper third. Optional B-cam side angle at 30–45°. Alternate punch-ins of about 110–130% between "wide" and "tight". Return to the frontal shot after B-roll to re-establish connection.
- **Editing techniques:** ruthless jump cuts (cut breaths, ums and dead air, which raises words per minute). Hide jump cuts with alternating punch-ins or an angle change. Cover with B-roll or graphics every 20–30s at most. For a talking-head-heavy video, target 30–50% B-roll/graphics coverage. Use B-roll that demonstrates, compares, proves or adds emotion, never generic B-roll. Use a cutout or green-screen subject over a graphic background for "explainer" moments. Use a picture-in-picture face bubble when showing screens.
- **Captions:** karaoke-style word-by-word captions in the lower-middle area, with keywords in a brand accent colour. Use a lower-third name and title once on LinkedIn.
- **Audio:** clean voice chain (denoise → EQ → compress → limiter). A subtle music bed, ducked hard or omitted for serious B2B. Light SFX on punch-ins only.
- **Mistakes:** opening with "Hi, I'm…"; a single static frame; punch-ins on every cut (seasick); B-roll that is too literal or generic; mismatched white balance between cameras.
- **References:** Alex Hormozi (rapid cuts, bold captions, B-roll inserts), Ali Abdaal (calm A/B-cam with graphics), Codie Sanchez, Justin Welsh (LinkedIn).

## 2. Podcast / webinar / interview clipping

- **Purpose:** turn one long recording into 5–15 stand-alone short assets (one hour usually yields 5–15 good clips).
- **Length:** 20–60s for TikTok/Reels/Shorts, 30–90s for LinkedIn (where slower, "executive" pacing is fine), 2–5 min for YouTube "mini-clips".
- **How to pick clips (virality signals):** OpusClip's model grades Hook / Flow / Value / Trend. Translated into a manual checklist:
  1. **Hook:** the clip starts on a complete, arresting sentence, never mid-thought. Bold claims, numbers, "the biggest mistake…", or a question.
  2. **Flow / self-containment:** one idea starts and finishes inside the clip. Reject moments that need more than 30s of unexplained setup. Watch for "same-speaker bias": a reaction without the question is meaningless.
  3. **Value:** a takeaway the viewer can carry away (tactic, number, reframe).
  4. **Emotion / quotability:** surprise, humour, conviction, controversy, a confession or a story.
  5. **Audio cues:** the speaker's pace speeds up, pitch varies, laughter or applause, a deliberate pause before a line.
  6. **Visual cues:** animated faces and gestures beat deadpan delivery.
- **Beat sheet:** (optional) a 1–2s text or spoken "hook card" re-stating the payoff, or reordering so the punchline plays first → setup → payoff → end on the strongest line (no fade-out trailing).
- **Shot grammar:** for multi-cam, cut to the active speaker, cut to reactions on L-cuts, and use stacked split-screen (host top, guest bottom) for 9:16 debates. Auto-reframe with face tracking for 16:9 → 9:16.
- **Techniques:** do a paper edit from the transcript first, which sources call non-negotiable for interview/podcast work. Tighten silences, use J/L cuts to the listener, punch-in on emphasis, and add B-roll or graphics for referenced things (stats, products, people).
- **Captions:** essential. Large, centred-low, active-word highlight. Add a speaker label or name lower-third at the start.
- **Audio:** level-match the speakers, remove room tone, and add little or no music (a light bed under the hook at most).
- **Mistakes:** clips starting mid-sentence; clips that end before the payoff; 16:9 letterboxed into 9:16 with blur bars; posting all clips at once instead of spreading them over the week.
- **References:** Diary of a CEO clips, Lex Fridman / Huberman clip channels, My First Million shorts, GaryVee's repurposing model.

## 3. Screen-recording product demo / SaaS walkthrough

- **Purpose:** show the product working, reducing perceived complexity. It is also used for changelog and feature posts.
- **Length:** 30–60s social feature demo, 60–120s landing-page demo, 2–5 min full walkthrough.
- **Beat sheet:** pain or outcome hook (0–4s) → "here's how" → 3–5 steps, one visual beat every 2–3s → the result or "aha" screen → CTA (URL or button).
- **Capture rules:** record at the highest resolution possible (2K/4K, 1:1 pixel ratio). Use 60fps when there is scrolling or drag. Set app zoom to 110–125%. Use a clean browser profile with no bookmarks, extensions or notifications, and seeded demo data. Move the cursor slower than feels natural and pause before and after every click.
- **Editing techniques (Screen Studio grammar):** auto-zoom to the click area (1.25×–2× typical, up to 5× for tiny UI). Smooth cursor path with eased glides, an enlarged cursor, click ripples or highlights, and motion blur on pans. Place the window on a branded gradient background with rounded corners and drop shadow. Speed-ramp through loading or typing. Spotlight or dim masking around the active element. Callout labels. Use a face-cam bubble in a corner for founder-led demos.
- **Higher-craft variant:** rebuild key screens as animated UI (vector recreation, with elements entering in 3D or isometric space) instead of raw capture. This is preferred for launch promos because it avoids the "pasted screenshot" look.
- **Captions:** step labels ("1. Connect GA4"), short benefit text, and VO captions if narrated.
- **Audio:** VO or music only. Soft UI clicks and whooshes on zooms. Duck music under VO.
- **Mistakes:** full-screen 1080p capture where text is unreadable on a phone; jittery cursor; showing every click; no zoom; dead time while pages load; real customer data visible.
- **References:** Linear changelog videos, Arc Browser, Raycast, Screen Studio's own demos, Loom/Tella creator demos.

## 4. Explainer / educational (value reels)

- **Purpose:** teach one concept and earn saves and shares. This is a top-of-funnel authority format.
- **Length:** 30–75s short, 2–5 min animated explainer, 8–20 min YouTube deep-dive.
- **Beat sheet:** knowledge-gap hook (a mistake, misconception, shortcut or surprising outcome) → why it matters → the insight, delivered early (don't save it for the outro) → example or proof (a visual) → recap line → save/share prompt.
- **Visual grammar:** diagrams, analogies made visual, step builds, before/after comparisons, annotated screenshots recreated as graphics, and data viz. One idea per screen.
- **Techniques:** progressive disclosure (build the diagram as the VO says each part), arrows and highlights, consistent colour coding (one colour = one concept across the whole video), match cuts between analogy and real thing, and Ken Burns on stills.
- **Captions:** VO captions plus a few keyword labels. Make sure they don't fight the diagram.
- **Audio:** clear VO and a calm, subtle music bed. Use SFX to punctuate builds (pops, ticks).
- **Mistakes:** too many ideas; a wall of text; payoff withheld until the end; a visual that doesn't match the spoken word.
- **References:** Kurzgesagt, Vox, Ali Abdaal, Wendover Productions, Ahrefs/HubSpot value reels.

## 5. Faceless channels (stock / AI B-roll + voiceover)

- **Purpose:** a scalable, privacy-preserving channel. Common niches are documentary, finance, history, tech, true crime and listicles.
- **Length:** 8–20 min long-form, 30–60s Shorts.
- **Beat sheet:** cold open with the most intriguing fact or question (0–15s) → promise → chapters, each with a mini-hook and payoff → re-hooks at about 25/50/75% → conclusion that resolves the opening loop.
- **Visual grammar:** a mixture is required. Footage, archival or news screenshots (recreated or animated), maps, text, charts and generated illustrations. Videos made almost entirely of generic stock footage underperform.
- **Techniques:** change the visual every 3–5s, apply Ken Burns or parallax to every still, push-ins on documents, map animations, kinetic headlines, and light grain or texture to unify mixed sources. Grade everything to one LUT so stock clips match.
- **Captions:** often burned in for Shorts. In long-form, use keyword pop-ups rather than full subtitles.
- **Audio:** VO quality is the product (human, or a good cloned voice). Use mood-matched music shifts per chapter and ambience or foley for realism.
- **Mistakes:** a monotone TTS voice; obviously random stock clips (a handshake for "partnership"); no structure; reused content with no transformation, which risks YouTube's "reused content" monetisation flag.
- **References:** MagnatesMedia, Business Casual, The Infographics Show, Lemmino (premium end).

## 6. UGC-style ads and paid social creative

- **Purpose:** native-feeling conversion creative. This is consistently among the top formats on Meta and TikTok.
- **Length:** 15–30s Meta, 21–34s TikTok, 6s bumper variants.
- **Beat sheet (4 beats):** **Hook (0–3s)**, often a "negative hook" (something looks wrong, a pain, a callout such as "If you run Shopify ads, stop…") → **Problem/agitation** in the viewer's own language → **Product as mechanism + one demonstrated result (3–20s)** → **CTA (20–30s)**, one single action.
- **Shot grammar:** selfie-cam handheld, natural light, real environments, product-in-hand, a "green screen" creator over a website or review screenshot, and reaction or unboxing shots. Storytelling visual hooks beat talking-head hooks (86:14 in one portfolio test).
- **Techniques:** fast jump cuts, native-app-style captions (the TikTok font look), on-screen text hook in the first frame, speed ramps on demos, split-screen us-vs-them, and before/after.
- **Iteration and testing system:** the hook is the highest-leverage element, so make 3–5 hook variants with the same body and CTA. In 2026 Meta (after the Andromeda update) rewards *whole-ad diversity*: vary hook, visual, copy, creator and format together rather than hook-only A/B tests. Once you have a winner, iterate the body, then the CTA. Build modularly: shoot multiple hooks, bodies and CTAs and assemble them as a matrix.
- **Audio:** the creator's natural voice and trending sounds (organic) or licensed tracks (ads). Sound on matters, so add captions too.
- **Mistakes:** looking like an ad in the first frame (polished logo intro); several messages in one ad; a CTA under the platform UI; using non-9:16.
- **References:** AG1, Ridge Wallet, Hims/Hers, Liquid Death (parody UGC), the Motion app ad library.

## 7. Product launch / promo

- **Purpose:** announce, create desire and drive sign-ups. It is an emotional plus a "what it is" film.
- **Length:** 60–90s hero (up to 120s), with 15–30s social cut-downs and 6s teasers.
- **Beat sheet:** Hook (0–4s: the pain or the bold promise) → tension or old way → reveal (logo or product moment) → demo montage (a visual beat every 2–3s, 3–5 key features) → outcome or transformation → social proof (optional) → lockup + CTA + date or URL. The value statement must land within 5–10s.
- **Visual grammar:** Apple/Linear/Stripe style uses restrained typography, a dark or premium palette, 3D or animated UI, macro "hero" product shots, camera moves through UI, masked reveals and match cuts.
- **Techniques:** beat-locked cuts to the music, speed ramps, kinetic type for the feature names, screen replacement, depth (parallax, z-space), light sweeps, and one signature transition motif repeated throughout.
- **Captions:** minimal, big, few words (feature names and the claim). If narrated, use subtitles in the social cuts only.
- **Audio:** a custom or licensed track with a clear build and drop at the reveal, plus tight sound design (whooshes, risers, UI ticks). VO is optional.
- **Mistakes:** leading with features rather than the problem; cramming in messages; generic stock; no CTA; no mobile or vertical cut.
- **References:** Linear, Arc (The Browser Company), Apple keynote product films, ElevenLabs, Stripe Sessions.

## 8. Motion graphics / kinetic typography

- **Purpose:** make words, data or abstract ideas visual. Used for brand manifestos, stat hits and lyric videos.
- **Length:** a whole kinetic-type piece should stay within 30–90s, because constant text motion tires viewers. Single "hits" run 5–15s.
- **Beat sheet:** sync to VO or music. Line → emphasis word → reset. Build to a peak phrase and a lockup.
- **Grammar and techniques:** 3–7 words per frame. Motion reinforces meaning (a "fall" drops, "grow" scales). Hierarchy through size and weight, with the power word in a heavy display face. Ease in and out (never linear). Use masking reveals, tracking expansion, per-character staggers, camera moves across a type layout, and shape morphs.
- **Readability:** keep a line on screen long enough to read at natural pace (about 0.3s per word plus 0.5–1s of buffer). Avoid thin weights, keep text inside the safe area, and keep contrast high.
- **Audio:** music-driven, with hits on beats and SFX on word pops.
- **Mistakes:** every word animated the same way; lines exiting before they're read; too many fonts; motion with no meaning.
- **References:** Apple "Shot on iPhone" type spots, Ordinary Folk, Buck, the Kurzgesagt intros.

## 9. Tutorials / how-to

- **Purpose:** the viewer completes a task. Search-driven and evergreen.
- **Length:** 30–60s short tips; 5–15 min full tutorials.
- **Beat sheet:** show the end result first (0–10s) → the "contract" (what you'll be able to do and how long it takes) → prerequisites → numbered steps (each step = goal → action → result check) → common pitfalls → recap → next video.
- **Grammar:** screen capture with zooms, overhead or hands shots for physical tasks, and a face cam for rapport.
- **Techniques:** granular chapters (one per step), an on-screen step counter ("Step 3/7"), zoom and highlight on each click, freeze-frame plus callout for important settings, speed-ramp repetitive actions, and split-screen "wrong vs right".
- **Captions:** step titles and labels for key values or shortcuts. Full captions for accessibility.
- **Audio:** clear VO and a very low or absent bed. A soft "ding" on step completion is optional.
- **Mistakes:** no step numbers or chapters; skipping a step; talking while the screen is idle; unreadable UI.
- **References:** Kevin Stratvert, Tiago Forte, Figma's official tutorials, Peter McKinnon (photo how-tos).

## 10. Listicles ("Top 5 / 7 mistakes")

- **Purpose:** packaged, scannable value. The number in the hook acts as a promise and a visible finish line.
- **Length:** 30–60s short (about 8–12s per item); 8–15 min long-form.
- **Beat sheet:** hook announcing the topic and count (tease the most surprising item) → stakes (≤10s) → items, ordered either as a countdown to #1 with escalating energy or with your strongest item first and second-strongest last → wrap or CTA.
- **Techniques:** a consistent "number card" transition (same motion every item, so it acts as a reset), a progress indicator, energy that escalates toward #1 (faster cuts, bigger SFX), and one hero visual per item.
- **Captions:** a persistent item title on screen (e.g., "#3 Wrong utm_source") plus spoken captions.
- **Audio:** a consistent item-transition SFX and a music build toward the end.
- **Mistakes:** a weak item in slot 1; inconsistent card design; padding items to hit a round number.
- **References:** WatchMojo, Ahrefs Shorts, the Hormozi "3 things" format.

## 11. Storytelling / mini-documentary

- **Purpose:** emotion, depth and brand narrative. Used for founder stories and visual journalism.
- **Length:** 3–8 min brand docs, 10–30 min YouTube docs, 60–90s micro-docs.
- **Beat sheet:** cold open in medias res (the most dramatic moment) → title → world and character → inciting problem → escalation and complication → turn → resolution → reflection or "why it matters". Use open loops throughout and re-hooks at quartiles.
- **Grammar:** interviews (two-angle, with an off-axis eyeline), verité B-roll, archival material, maps and timelines, and a texture-rich graphic language (paper, grain, grids).
- **Techniques:** paper edit from transcripts (chronological order is rarely optimal); J/L cuts to carry interview audio over B-roll; map animations and pin-drops (Johnny Harris style); split-screen documents; push-ins on photos; rack focus; let silence and stillness land (2–3s pauses after big lines, once or twice per video).
- **Captions:** lower-thirds for names and places, highlighted documents, and minimal subtitles.
- **Audio:** low drones and rhythmic ticks under narration, crisp foley (paper rustle, typewriter, camera shutter on text pops), and music that follows emotional energy (sparse → build → full at the payoff).
- **Mistakes:** chronological info-dumping; no character; music wall-to-wall; no open loop.
- **References:** Johnny Harris, Vox (Borders), Lemmino, Patagonia films, Stripe Press video essays.

## 12. Testimonials / case studies

- **Purpose:** social proof that reduces risk. Strongest for B2B or for a complex sale.
- **Length:** 30–60s social cut, 1.5–3 min full case study, plus 10–15s quote cuts for ads.
- **Beat sheet (Problem–Solution–Results):** who they are (5s) → the problem, quantified and emotional (how long it took, what it cost) → why they chose you → solution in action (product B-roll) → **results with numbers** → recommendation line (often reused as the hook).
- **Grammar:** interview at an off-camera eyeline, in their real workplace, with B-roll of the team and product in use, and stat cards.
- **Techniques:** open with the strongest quote or result (a flash-forward), then rewind. Cover cuts with B-roll, animate number callouts (count-ups), and use a lower-third with name, title, company and logo.
- **Captions:** full captions plus highlighted key phrases.
- **Audio:** an authentic voice, an optimistic but restrained bed, and room tone matched across cuts.
- **Mistakes:** scripted, stilted delivery (genuine beats polished); vague results with no numbers; too long; the brand logo dominating.
- **References:** Slack and HubSpot customer stories, Notion "Made with Notion", Gong case videos.

## 13. Before/after and transformations

- **Purpose:** an instant visual payoff, ideal for makeovers, design, fitness, data cleanup or UI redesigns.
- **Length:** 7–20s (built for loops), up to 30–45s with process shots.
- **Beat sheet:** "before" state plus a tension caption ("this was a mess") → optional process montage, speed-ramped → **reveal on the beat drop** → hold on the "after" → loop back.
- **Techniques:** hand-cover, snap or clap transitions; whip pan; the beat-drop cut (the subject changes decisively on the downbeat); a wipe or slider reveal (draggable split line); split-screen side by side; morph or match cut on identical framing (lock the camera position).
- **Captions:** "Before" / "After" labels and an outcome metric.
- **Audio:** a trending or energetic track with a clear drop, plus a riser into the reveal.
- **Mistakes:** different framing or lighting between before and after (feels fake); reveal off-beat; too much process.
- **References:** Canva, home-renovation creators, Duolingo-style UI redesign posts, Figma "redesign" reels.

## 14. Memes / trend formats

- **Purpose:** reach, relatability and brand personality.
- **Length:** 5–20s.
- **Beat sheet:** the trend's native template (a POV caption, a "me when…" setup, or a trending audio structure) applied with a brand-specific twist.
- **Techniques:** native-app look (TikTok text styles, low production), lip-sync to the trending audio, a reaction cut-in, freeze-frame plus record scratch, zoom-punch on the punchline, and green-screen over a screenshot.
- **Speed matters:** trends are short-lived. Ship within days and skip anything that is already saturated. Meme campaigns reportedly draw about 60% organic engagement versus about 5% for branded graphics.
- **Captions:** the POV text is the joke. Keep it short, at the top-middle inside the safe zone.
- **Audio:** the trending sound for organic (copyright rules differ for business accounts, which must use the Commercial Music Library). Original audio can become a trend.
- **Mistakes:** over-produced; joining late; a brand joke that requires product knowledge; an off-brand tone.
- **References:** Duolingo (the "unhinged" owl), Ryanair (the talking plane), Scrub Daddy, Ahrefs memes.

## 15. Carousels-to-video

- **Purpose:** reuse proven static carousels as Reels or TikToks (completion rate is a stronger ranking signal than saves).
- **Length:** 15–45s.
- **Beat sheet:** slide 1 (the hook) becomes the first frame, with motion → each slide becomes a 2–4s beat → the final slide becomes the CTA. Make sure the payoff lands within the first 80% of runtime.
- **Techniques:** design 9:16 natively (no cropping 4:5). Animate slide elements in (staggered builds, not whole-slide cuts), use push and slide transitions that mimic swiping, keyframe the text in sync with the VO, and add a progress bar.
- **Text:** rewrite carousel copy "for the ear" if narrated. Otherwise give each beat enough time to read (about 0.3s per word plus a buffer).
- **Audio:** a trending or beat track with cuts on beats, or a VO.
- **Mistakes:** a static slideshow with a crossfade; too much text per slide; the auto-convert button with no re-pacing.
- **References:** Instagram's "Create Reel from post" feature, PostNitro templates, Justin Welsh's and Sahil Bloom's carousel→video posts.

## 16. Long-form YouTube (and repurposing into Shorts)

- **Purpose:** depth, search, binge-watching, monetisation and trust.
- **Length:** 8–20 min typical. A 60–70% drop-off in the first 30s is common, so the opening is everything.
- **Structure (5-block spine):** **Cold open** (0:00–0:15: the sharpest line plus a visual proving the promise) → **Contract** (0:15–0:45: what you'll get and roughly how long) → **3–5 body blocks**, each self-contained with its own mini-hook, payoff and a visual reset at the boundary → **re-hooks** scripted at about 25%, 50% and 75% → **Outro** (15–20s max, tied to the opening promise, pointing to the next video). MrBeast-style editing treats each chapter as a mini-cliffhanger.
- **Chapters:** map them to real beat transitions. Use descriptive titles readable in under 2s ("The second method", not "Part 2"). Use granular chapters for tutorials and broader ones for mystery or story content.
- **B-roll density:** a 30–50% runtime target. Tier 1 is purpose-shot footage (aim for 60%+ of the B-roll), Tier 2 is stock, Tier 3 is graphics, text and screen captures.
- **Five-pass workflow:** Assembly (10%) → Rough cut (30%) → Fine cut with B-roll, text, chapters, temp music and SFX (35%) → Polish: grade, mix, graphics (15%) → QC on the target device, captions and chapters (10%).
- **Retention diagnostics:** a steep first-30s drop means the hook didn't match the packaging. A gradual decline means pacing fatigue. A mid-video cliff means a tangent or sponsor read. A sawtooth means uneven section quality.
- **Repurposing into Shorts:** use the clip-picking checklist in §2. Re-hook the first second (re-record or reorder). Reframe to 9:16 with face tracking. Recaption. Cut to 15–45s and end on the payoff or a loop. Optionally add a "full video" related-video link.
- **References:** MrBeast, Veritasium, Colin and Samir, Ali Abdaal, MKBHD.

## 17. Livestream highlights

- **Purpose:** extend a live moment's reach, both for gaming and business (AMAs, launches).
- **Length:** 15–60s (Twitch clips run 5–60s); YouTube compilations run 8–15 min.
- **Beat sheet:** a 1–3s context caption ("Chat bet he couldn't…") → build → the moment (reaction peak) → aftermath or reaction tail → cut.
- **Layout for 9:16:** face cam on the top ~third and gameplay or screen on the bottom two-thirds, keeping the action centred. Alternatives are side-by-side or a PiP face bubble. Remove or relocate the chat box and alerts.
- **Techniques:** zoom on the face for reactions, freeze-frame plus SFX before the payoff, replay in slow motion, and meme insert graphics. Captions are mandatory because live audio is messy.
- **Audio:** normalise game versus voice and duck game audio under speech. Watch out for DMCA music in game or stream audio.
- **Mistakes:** 16:9 with blurred bars; too much pre-roll context; chat-dependent jokes left unexplained.
- **References:** Kai Cenat and xQc clip channels, Ludwig, and Apple/Google event highlight cuts.

## 18. Event recaps

- **Purpose:** FOMO for next year, sponsor value and community proof.
- **Length:** 60–120s social (vertical), 2–4 min hero recap; 15–30s teaser.
- **Beat sheet:** hook with the loudest reaction or most visual shot (0–10s) → build: arrival, atmosphere, scale (10–30s) → key moments: speakers, product, crowd peaks (30–60s+) → emotional or quote beat → finale: group shot, chant, logo plus next-year date.
- **Shot list:** wides for scale, crowd reactions and faces enjoying themselves, speakers and VIPs, details (badges, swag, signage), behind the scenes, plus one or two short soundbites.
- **Techniques:** build a silent assembly first, labelling each clip's job (setup, momentum, surprise, emotional pause, payoff, outro), then pick music. Use one song, place key moments on its high points, use speed ramps and match cuts between attendees, and keep transitions simple (pace over effects).
- **Captions:** location, date and stat cards ("2,400 attendees"), plus subtitles on the soundbites.
- **Audio:** one licensed song with rights cleared, with natural sound pops (applause, laughter) layered at the peaks.
- **Mistakes:** chronological order; too many speaker clips; transitions everywhere; unlicensed music.
- **References:** Web Summit, Config by Figma, INBOUND, Apple WWDC recap reels.

## 19. B2B / LinkedIn video

- **Purpose:** thought leadership, pipeline and employer brand.
- **Length:** organic 30–90s (under 60s is safest); ads under 15s for most winners (they can run up to 30 min).
- **Formats that work:** founder talking-head insight, customer clip, "whiteboard" explainer, product micro-demo, event and webinar clips, behind-the-scenes, and data-insight motion graphics.
- **Beat sheet:** a professional-problem hook (a stat or contrarian insight) → the insight with one example or proof → a takeaway framework → a soft CTA (comment, follow, or a demo for ads).
- **Specs:** vertical 9:16 for the vertical video feed, or 4:5 (1080×1350) for feed and ads, plus square. Use burned-in captions plus an SRT for ads.
- **Techniques:** calmer pacing than TikTok (punch-ins, a few graphics, minimal SFX), a branded but restrained caption style, and lower-thirds with name and title (credibility). Authentic, native delivery beats polish.
- **Mistakes:** over-produced corporate tone; logo intros; consumer-meme pacing; no captions.
- **References:** Gong, HubSpot, Chris Walker (Refine Labs), Goldcast clips, and the Justin Welsh / Jasmin Alić creator style.

## 20. Core editing techniques glossary (what it is, and when to use it)

| Technique | What it is / when to use it |
|---|---|
| **Jump cut** | A cut within the same shot to remove dead time. Use it for talking-head pacing, hidden by a punch-in or B-roll. |
| **J-cut** | The next shot's audio starts before its picture. Use it to pull the viewer into a new scene or speaker and to build anticipation. |
| **L-cut** | The current audio continues over the next picture. Use it to show reactions or B-roll while someone keeps talking; it is a VO's best friend. |
| **Match cut** | Cut between two shots that share a shape, motion or composition. Use it for time jumps, analogy-to-reality links and transformations. |
| **Whip pan** | A fast blurred pan used as a transition. Use it for energetic scene changes, before/after and travel; it can be hidden in the blur. |
| **Speed ramp** | Speed changes smoothly within a clip (fast→slow). Use it to emphasise a moment on the beat, to skip boring motion, or for product hero shots. |
| **Punch-in (digital zoom)** | A 110–130% scale-up on the cut. Use it to emphasise a line, hide a jump cut, or add a comedic beat. Don't use it on every cut. |
| **Split screen** | Two or more frames at once. Use it for comparisons (vs, before/after), conversations, and podcasts in 9:16. |
| **Picture-in-picture** | A small inset frame (face bubble). Use it for founder-led screen demos, reactions and livestream layouts. |
| **Green screen / AI cutout** | Isolate the subject and place it over a new background. Use it for creator-over-screenshot commentary, explainers and "walk-through-the-UI" shots. |
| **Masking** | Reveal or hide parts of a frame. Use it for text-behind-subject, object reveals, wipe transitions and spotlighting UI. |
| **Freeze frame** | Hold a single frame. Use it to introduce a character with a label, for a "record scratch" comedic beat, or to call out a detail. |
| **Rack focus** | Shift focus between planes (in camera or simulated with blur). Use it to redirect attention in docs, product shots and reveals. |
| **Ken Burns** | Slow pan or zoom on a still. Use it to give life to photos, screenshots and archival material in documentaries and faceless videos. |
| **Glitch** | Digital distortion and RGB split. Use it for tech or edgy themes and "error" moments, briefly. |
| **Light leaks / film burns** | Organic colour flares. Use them for warm or nostalgic transitions in recaps, lifestyle and wedding films, sparingly. |
| **Kinetic type** | Animated typography. Use it for hooks, key claims, manifestos and stat callouts, with motion matching meaning. |
| **Map animation** | Zoom, route lines, region highlights. Use it for geography, expansion stories, logistics and documentaries. |
| **Data viz** | Animated charts and count-ups. Use it to make numbers memorable in case studies, explainers and reports. Keep one insight per chart. |
| **Screen-recording zoom** | Auto or keyframed zoom to the action area plus a smooth cursor. Use it for every UI demo and tutorial, because readability on mobile depends on it. |
| **Auto-reframe** | AI subject tracking to change aspect ratio (16:9→9:16/4:5). Use it to repurpose long-form into shorts and to make multi-platform versions. |
| **Color grading / LUTs** | Correct (balance, exposure, matching) then grade (look). Use LUTs to unify mixed sources and match cameras. Check skin tones and grade for mobile screens. |
| **Audio ducking** | Automatically lower music under speech (6–12 dB auto, or −15 to −20 dB under dialogue). Use it on any VO or dialogue over music. Use attack 30–80ms and release 250–700ms to avoid pumping. |
| **Sound design / SFX** | Whooshes, risers, pops and foley. Use them to give transitions and text pops weight, at most once every 20–40s in calm formats. |
| **Beat sync** | Cut on musical beats or downbeats. Use it for promos, recaps, before/after and montages. Place the hero moment on the drop. |
| **Lower third** | Name, title or location graphic. Use it for interviews, testimonials and B2B credibility, the first time a person appears. |
| **Cutaway / B-roll** | Supporting footage over the A-roll. Use it to illustrate, prove, cover edits and reset attention. |
| **Parallax / 2.5D** | Separated layers moving at different speeds. Use it to add depth to stills in documentaries and faceless videos. |
| **Motion blur** | Blur on fast motion. Use it on any animated UI or graphic move so it feels filmed, not "slid". |
| **Loop edit** | The last frame flows into the first. Use it for Shorts and Reels, because replays are weighted heavily. |

## 21. Decision cheatsheet for an autonomous agent

1. **Identify the type** (from the sections above), then load its beat sheet, length and audio rules.
2. **Pick the platform matrix:** produce a master (16:9 or 9:16), then cut-downs (9:16 15–34s, 4:5 <15s for LinkedIn ads, 1:1 if needed). Check safe zones for every version.
3. **Hook check:** is there something spoken or visually arresting at frame 1? Is there no logo or intro card first?
4. **Rhythm check:** is cut density varied (3–7s base, 1–2s peaks, a few 8–12s holds)? Is there no more than 20–30s of an unbroken static frame?
5. **Caption check:** are captions burned in, inside the safe zone, not overlapping graphics, using a bold weight?
6. **Audio check:** is speech intelligible, music ducked 12–20 dB under speech, output −14 LUFS with true peak ≤ −1 dBTP, and SFX used sparingly?
7. **Payoff check:** does the core value land within the first 80% of runtime? Does it end on the strongest line or loop, with no outro card on shorts?
8. **QC:** watch it on a phone at small size, both sound off and sound on.
